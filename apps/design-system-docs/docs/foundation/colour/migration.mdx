Two generations of EDS colour are in use, and each needs a different route to the redefined tokens.
Neither is a find-and-replace, so this page starts with what changed and why.

The most useful thing to know before you start: **the redefined scale is contrast-driven, not
lightness-driven.** Picking the closest hex to what you have today is the wrong mental model and will
give you a palette that fails contrast in one scheme or the other. Choose by role instead, and let
[the pairings](./getting_started.mdx#foreground-pairing) do the rest.

Work is tracked in
[equinor/design-system#5399](https://github.com/equinor/design-system/issues/5399).

## Coming from EDS 1.x

1.x named colours by **component role**, in a flat set of hand-picked hex values:
`colors.interactive.primary__resting`, `colors.text.static_icons__default`,
`colors.ui.background__light`. There were 75 colour tokens in five groups, and states were suffixes
(`__resting`, `__hover`, `__selected`).

The redefined set names colours by **surface, behaviour, tone, level and state**, and there are
considerably more of them. That means a 1.x token usually has several possible answers rather than
one, and which is right depends on what you were colouring. A single `primary__resting` might become
a button fill, a link, a selected row or an icon, and each takes a different token.

So the route from 1.x is to work from **what you were building**, not from the old name.
[Getting started](./getting_started.mdx) has worked examples for the common cases, and
[Introduction](./intro.mdx) explains how to read a name so you can derive the rest.

Two groups have no direct route:

- **`infographic`** becomes `data-visualization.*`, which is a different palette rather than a
  rename. Re-pick your series colours; [the palette](./palette.mdx#data-visualisation) describes what
  is available.
- **`logo`** has no equivalent in the colour scale.

## Coming from 2.0.0-beta

The beta shares the redefined set's vocabulary, so this route is closer to a translation. `emphasis`
and `muted` already exist, tones already exist, and fill, border and text are already separated. What
changes is the shape of the names and one removed feature.

### Dynamic colour is removed

The `Appearance` collection and appearance-switching at the semantic-colour level are gone. There is
one approach now: semantic colour, where the tone is part of the token name.

If you relied on a generic role token resolving to a different tone depending on context, name the
tone explicitly instead. If you have `data-color-appearance` in your markup, or appearance modes in
your Figma file, those come out.

### Names are reordered

The beta put the tone before the surface word and used `fill`. The redefined set puts the surface
first and drops `fill`:

```
--eds-color-bg-accent-fill-emphasis-hover        beta
--eds-background-interactive-accent-emphasis-hover   redefined
```

The interactive and non-interactive split is also new, and it is the part worth reading about before
you start. [Introduction](./intro.mdx#2-interactive-can-the-user-act-on-it) covers it.

### `active` becomes `pressed`

The state word changed, because `active` was read as "currently on" about as often as "being
pressed".

## Checking your work

APCA contrast against `background.surface` is the acceptance criterion, and the pairings are built to
meet it. If you use a fill with the foregrounds it is specified for, contrast is already handled. See
[Accessibility](./intro.mdx#accessibility-is-built-in).
