Two generations of EDS colour are in use, and each needs a different route. This page starts with the
one thing that decides how you approach either of them.

:::warning No value survived the redefinition
Not one of the 115 semantic colours in 2.0.0-beta resolves to the same value as any redefined token,
and the 1.x palette was replaced outright. The scale is now **contrast-driven rather than
lightness-driven**, so matching your old colours by eye, or by nearest hex, gives you a palette that
fails contrast in one scheme or the other.

Map by **role**: what was this colouring, and what was it doing. The pairs below are matched that
way, and the swatches show how large a change to expect.
:::

Work is tracked in
[equinor/design-system#5399](https://github.com/equinor/design-system/issues/5399).

## Coming from EDS 1.x

1.x named colours by **component role** in a flat set of hand-picked hex values:
`colors.interactive.primary__resting`, `colors.text.static_icons__default`. There were 75 tokens in
five groups, and states were suffixes: `__resting`, `__hover`, `__selected`, `__activated`.

The redefined set names colours by **surface, behaviour, tone, level and state**. That is five
questions where 1.x asked one, which is why a single old token often has more than one right answer.
[Introduction](./intro.mdx#reading-a-token-name) explains how to read a name, and it is worth reading
before you start rather than after.

### Text

`static_icons__*` covered both text and icons. The redefined set separates them, because a thin icon
stroke needs more contrast than a letterform of the same size. Everything below has an `icon.*` twin.

<MigrationOneText />

### Backgrounds and surfaces

The `ui` group was a small set of planes plus three status tints. The planes map cleanly. The status tints
become non-interactive tone fills, which is the group for anything that carries meaning but cannot be
clicked.

<MigrationOneUi />

### Interactive colours

The largest group, and the one that changes shape most. Three things to know before reading it:

- **`primary` and `secondary` are not tones.** They described a button's rank. The redefined set uses
  `accent` for the primary action and `neutral` for the secondary one, and rank is expressed by
  choosing `emphasis` or `muted`.
- **`__resting` is now `default`, and `__activated` is now `pressed`.** `active` was read as
  "currently on" about as often as "being pressed".
- **`__highlight` was doing two different jobs**, a muted tint and a selection state. Those are now
  `muted` and `selected`, and only `accent` and `neutral` have `selected`.

<MigrationOneInteractive />

### Table colours

1.x shipped table fills as their own tokens. The redefined set has no component-specific colour: a
table cell is a surface, and a hovered row is an interactive neutral fill. Use the same tokens you
would use anywhere else.

<MigrationOneTable />

### Infographic and logo

`infographic` (24 tokens) becomes `data-visualization.*`, but this is a **re-palette rather than a
rename**. The redefined set has ten categorical hues of five steps each, plus a sequential and a
diverging ramp, and none of them corresponds to an old `substitute__*` or `primary__*` value. Re-pick
your series colours from [the palette](./palette.mdx#data-visualisation).

`logo` (2 tokens) has no equivalent. Brand marks are not part of the colour scale.

## Coming from 2.0.0-beta

This route is closer to a translation. `emphasis` and `muted` already exist, tones already exist, and
fill, border and text are already separated. Four things change.

### Dynamic colour is removed

The `Appearance` collection and appearance-switching at the semantic-colour level are gone. There is
one approach now: semantic colour, where the tone is part of the token name.

If you relied on a generic role token resolving to a different tone depending on context, name the
tone explicitly. If you have `data-color-appearance` in your markup, or appearance modes in your
Figma file, those come out.

### Names are reordered, and `fill` is dropped

The beta put the tone before the surface word and used `fill`. The redefined set puts the surface
first, drops `fill`, and adds the interactive split:

```
--eds-color-bg-accent-fill-emphasis-hover             2.0.0-beta
--eds-background-interactive-accent-emphasis-hover    redefined
```

### The interactive split is new

This is the part that needs a decision rather than a rename. In the beta, a tinted banner and a
tinted button could share `--eds-color-bg-accent-fill-muted-default`. They cannot now: the button is
`background.interactive.*` and carries hover and pressed values, the banner is
`background.non-interactive.*` and has none.

So when you meet a `fill` token, ask whether the thing it colours responds to input.
[Introduction](./intro.mdx#2-interactive-can-the-user-act-on-it) covers the distinction.

### Text

Tone-coloured text is now named for **what it sits on** rather than for how strong it is. That is what
makes the pairing checkable: `text.on-muted.accent` is the foreground for an accent muted fill, and
nothing else needs to be worked out.

## Checking your work

APCA contrast against `background.surface` is the acceptance criterion, and the pairings are built to
meet it. If you use a fill with the foregrounds it is specified for, contrast is already handled. See
[Accessibility](./intro.mdx#accessibility-is-built-in) and
[Foreground pairing](./getting_started.mdx#foreground-pairing).
