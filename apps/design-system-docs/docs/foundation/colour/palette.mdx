This page is about the layer underneath the tokens. You do not use it — the scale is hidden from the
Figma picker and is not part of the public token set. Read it when you want to know *why* a token
looks the way it does, or when you are proposing a new one.

## Colour is generated, not picked

Every value in the scale is produced by a formula from three inputs: a **hue anchor**, a
**lightness** value, and a **chroma** curve. Nobody chooses individual colours.

There are seven hue anchors — blue, gray, green, moss green, north sea, orange and red — each defined
once in **OKLCH**. OKLCH is used because changing hue in it does not change perceived lightness, so
every tone's step 9 reads as equally solid whatever colour it is.

Chroma follows a bell curve across the scale rather than being set per step, which keeps the middle
of the scale saturated and both ends muted without anyone tuning it.

The practical consequence: **adding a seventh tone is not a design exercise.** Point it at a hue
anchor and the whole scale follows.

## What the step numbers mean

Each tone has 15 steps. The obvious reading — "1 is lightest, 15 is darkest" — is wrong.

A step number is a **role position**, not a lightness. Step 9 is "the solid fill". Step 15 is "the
foreground that sits on a solid fill". That is true in both schemes, and it is what lets one set of
token names work in light and dark with no overrides.

Steps 1 → 13 broadly travel from *closest to the background* toward *furthest from it*, which means
descending lightness in light mode and ascending in dark.

:::warning The scale is deliberately not monotonic
"Broadly" is doing real work in that sentence. In dark mode the lightness values **dip at steps 4 and
5** on purpose: a strictly increasing ramp could not hold the contrast that the border and muted-fill
roles at those steps need.

So do not treat global ordering as a rule, and do not "fix" the dip. The two rules that do hold are
narrower:

1. **No two steps within one scale resolve to the same value.**
2. **Every state ladder progresses visibly in one direction.**

Steps 14 and 15 are excluded from both — see below.
:::

## Which step does what

Read this as the map between the scale and the tokens you actually use.

| Step | Role |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | canvas, input fills, and the resting muted fill |
| 2 | muted fill on hover; the disabled fill |
| 3 | muted fill when pressed |
| 4 | muted borders, the disabled border, neutral selected hover |
| 5 | accent selected; muted border on hover |
| 6 | *no consumer* |
| 7 | muted border when pressed; backdrop; tertiary foreground |
| 8 | links; secondary foreground; accent selected pressed |
| 9 | **the solid fill**; emphasis borders |
| 10 | solid fill on hover; `on-default` foregrounds |
| 11 | solid fill when pressed; interactive foregrounds |
| 12 | `on-muted` foregrounds; the inverted surface |
| 13 | primary foreground; the selected indicator |
| 14 | *no consumer* |
| 15 | the content plane; `on-emphasis` foregrounds |

### Steps 14 and 15 are inverse-polarity

They sit outside the ladder by design. Both are **foreground values meant to sit *on* a solid fill**,
so their polarity is reversed relative to steps 1–13 and they must be excluded from any ordering
check.

Step 15 does double duty: it is also the content plane, which is why `background.surface`,
`background.dialog` and `background.floating` all resolve to it.

### Steps 6 and 14 have no consumer

They are not spare capacity waiting to be used. Inserting or repurposing a step renames everything
above it, which breaks every consumer at once — so either they are given roles or they are dropped
before the scale is settled. Treat "just use step 6" as a proposal, not a fix.

## The steps jump on purpose

The scale is not a smooth gradient. Lightness values are chosen per step so that the pairings that
matter — text against surface, fill against canvas — land *at* the contrast target rather than near
it.

Notice that the middle of the scale is evenly spaced while the run from borders into fills jumps
roughly twice as far. That gap is where strokes stop and fills begin, and it needs to be visible.

:::note The lightness inputs are hand-tuned, and that is deliberate
A purely formulaic lightness ramp was tried and did not produce usable contrast steps. The values are
tuned by hand; the algorithm's job is to propagate them across hues, not to invent them. This is not
technical debt and should not be "corrected" by deriving the ramp from a formula.
:::

## Tone to hue

The mapping from a tone to a hue happens per scheme, which is what allows `neutral` to be a different
hue in each:

| Tone | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accent` | Moss green | Moss green |
| `neutral` | **Gray** | **North sea** |
| `info` | Blue | Blue |
| `success` | Green | Green |
| `warning` | Orange | Orange |
| `danger` | Red | Red |

Dark-mode neutral is North sea rather than grey on purpose: a pure-grey dark interface reads as dead,
and the slight blue keeps large dark surfaces feeling like a material.

## Data visualisation

`data-visualization.*` is a separate group with its own rules, because chart colour is about telling
series apart rather than about contrast against a background.

- `cat.1` … `cat.10`, five steps each — categorical hues
- `seq.1` … `seq.7` — a sequential ramp for magnitude
- `div.1` … `div.9` — a diverging ramp for values around a midpoint

These are the only values in the set that sit outside the OKLCH generator, and no EDS component binds
them — they are for you to apply in charts. Guidance on ordering and accessibility is still being
written.

## Why you cannot extend the scale yourself

Inserting a step shifts every position above it and renames the whole scale, which silently breaks
every consumer. So the scale is treated as fixed: an expanded scale ships alongside the current one
rather than replacing it, and consumers move at their own pace.

If you need a value the scale does not have, that is a token request rather than something to
generate locally.
